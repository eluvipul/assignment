## Chi-square test of independence: App choice vs. payment method

We’ll use the contingency table from Task 1, containing 24 simulated survey responses, to test whether users’ favorite food delivery app (Zomato, Swiggy, or Uber Eats) is associated with their preferred payment method (Paytm, PhonePe, or Credit Card).

### 1. Observed frequency table

| Favorite app | Paytm | PhonePe | Credit Card | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Zomato | 4 | 3 | 2 | 9 |
| Swiggy | 3 | 4 | 2 | 9 |
| Uber Eats | 2 | 2 | 2 | 6 |
| Total | 9 | 9 | 6 | 24 |

### 2. State the hypotheses

- Null hypothesis ($H_0$): Favorite app choice and preferred payment method are independent; there is no association between them.
- Alternative hypothesis ($H_1$): Favorite app choice and preferred payment method are associated.

We’ll use a significance level of $\alpha = 0.05$.

### 3. Calculate the expected frequencies

The formula for each cell is:

$$
E=\frac{\text{Row total}\times\text{Column total}}{\text{Grand total}}
$$

For example, the expected number of Zomato users preferring Paytm is:

$$
E=\frac{9\times9}{24}=3.375
$$

Applying the formula to all cells gives:

| Favorite app | Paytm | PhonePe | Credit Card |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Zomato | 3.375 | 3.375 | 2.250 |
| Swiggy | 3.375 | 3.375 | 2.250 |
| Uber Eats | 2.250 | 2.250 | 1.500 |

### 4. Calculate the chi-square statistic

The test statistic is calculated as:

$$
\chi^2=\sum\frac{(O-E)^2}{E}
$$

where $O$ is the observed frequency and $E$ is the expected frequency.

For example, for the Zomato–Paytm cell:

$$
\frac{(4-3.375)^2}{3.375}=0.1157
$$

Summing the contributions from all nine cells gives:

$$
\boxed{\chi^2\approx0.593}
$$

The degrees of freedom are:

$$
df=(r-1)(c-1)=(3-1)(3-1)=4
$$

### 5. Results and decision

Chi-square statistic

## 0.593

Degrees of freedom

## 4

Approximate p-value

## 0.964

Significance level: 0.05

Decision: Fail to reject the null hypothesis.

Since the p-value ($0.964$) is greater than $0.05$, we do not have sufficient evidence of an association between favorite app and preferred payment method.

### 6. Python verification

python

```

import numpy as np
from scipy.stats import chi2_contingency

observed = np.array([
    [4, 3, 2],  # Zomato
    [3, 4, 2],  # Swiggy
    [2, 2, 2]   # Uber Eats
])

chi2, p, dof, expected = chi2_contingency(
    observed, correction=False
)

print("Expected frequencies:")
print(np.round(expected, 3))
print("Chi-square statistic:", round(chi2, 3))
print("Degrees of freedom:", dof)
print("P-value:", round(p, 4))
```

Important limitation: Several expected cell counts are below 5, so the usual chi-square approximation may not be reliable for this small sample. An exact test for a $3\times3$ table or a larger sample would be preferable for a stronger conclusion. The result above is based on simulated survey data, not actual customer behavior.

## Chi-square goodness of fit test: Flipkart product launch

We want to test whether the survey results support the company's claim that 70% of users will prefer the new product.

### 1. State the hypotheses

- Null hypothesis ($H_0$): 70% of users prefer the new product ($p=0.70$).
- Alternative hypothesis ($H_1$): The preference proportion differs from 70% ($p\neq0.70$).

Assume a significance level of $\alpha=0.05$.

### 2. Calculate the expected counts

Total users surveyed = 30.

- Expected users who prefer the product: $30\times0.70=21$
- Expected users who do not prefer it: $30\times0.30=9$

| Response | Observed count ($O$) | Expected count ($E$) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prefer | 18 | 21 |
| Do not prefer | 12 | 9 |
| Total | 30 | 30 |

### 3. Calculate the chi-square statistic

The formula is:

$$
\chi^2=\sum\frac{(O-E)^2}{E}
$$

Substituting the values:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\chi^2
&=\frac{(18-21)^2}{21}
+\frac{(12-9)^2}{9}\\
&=\frac{9}{21}+\frac{9}{9}\\
&=0.4286+1\\
&=\boxed{1.429}
\end{aligned}
$$

Degrees of freedom:

$$
df=k-1=2-1=1
$$

### 4. Decision and conclusion

Chi-square statistic

## 1.429

Degrees of freedom

## 1

p-value

## 0.232

Significance level

## 0.05

Since $p=0.232>0.05$, we fail to reject the null hypothesis.

Conclusion: The survey does not provide sufficient statistical evidence to reject Flipkart's claim that 70% of users prefer the new product. This does not prove that the claim is true; a larger sample could provide more conclusive evidence.
